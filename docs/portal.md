# Capsid Portal

Capsid Portal is the administrator's view of Capsid, one app at https://portal.dustinedwards.info (and at `/portal/` on the Worker's workers.dev address until the new one is verified live): every site, the job queue across every namespace, the watcher's findings, deploys, agents, namespaces, activity, claims, backups and CI. The designs and their rulings are in Capsid at `capsid/research/design-ops-console.md` and `capsid/research/design-portal-unify.md`.

**It moved from `/console` with no redirects.** Until the move, a server-rendered summary page answered at `/console` and this app at `/console/app/`. The move deleted that page, after every part of it had a replacement here (the design's section 5), and every `/console` address now answers the Worker's plain 404. A browser signed in at `/console` signs in once more at `/portal/`, because the old cookies were scoped to `Path=/console`.

**Its controls are the registry in `src/controls.ts`**, each on the row it changes:
- the job drawer: resume a blocked job (with an optional note, the full approval the driver reads as `resume_note.note`; a blocked job that asks a question offers it as Answer), close a blocked job as shipped, release a claimed one, mark a job failed, supersede a queued job or a claimed one with no work recorded (`supersede_job`, with a reason; no outcome is written against anybody), park a queued job with a one-line reason saying when it comes back, and unpark a parked one (`park_job` and `unpark_job`, each other's reverse, so the result offers Undo);
- chat only for now, through the `capsid_admin_run_controls` tool: approve pull requests at the head reviewed (`approve_pr`, docs/autonomy.md, "The approval record"); it records and never merges, and its Portal screens are the merge pipeline's inbox PR;
- chat only for now, through the same tool: promote a merge unit to auto or remove one by signing the next auto-merge policy version, with that version number typed as confirm (`sign_policy_change`), lift a unit's suspension after a rollback (`unit_resume`) (docs/autonomy.md, "Earned promotion and demotion"), and lift a namespace's auto-merge pause after bad unattended merges (`automerge_resume`, with a reason) (docs/autonomy.md, "The safety meter, the shutoff, and existing tests");
- the Pull requests page, its Auto-merge section, the Auto-merge policy panel: whether the signed policy loads, and "Load from repo and sign" (`sign_policy_from_repo`, the file's version typed as confirm), which writes `docs/policy/auto-merge.md` from the default branch to the store and signs it (docs/autonomy.md, "Signing the policy from the repo");
- Needs you and the Pull requests page: close a pull request waiting for you without merging it (`pr_close`, with a reason), as `capsid_close_pr` action `close` does, head-branch cleanup included; GitHub can reopen it, the Portal does not;
- the agent drawer: revoke an agent;
- Settings, in its Automation and namespaces section: the Automation panel, with the overnight run's switch and its "Runs on" choice (choosing Subscription records Dustin's decision, its date and its reasoning, docs/overnight.md); and reset the queue's circuit breaker when it is open. The improve loop's switch and the per-namespace pause switches went with the loop, removed 2026-10-19;
- the Settings view: add, edit and remove a site, and add, edit and remove a package;
- Needs you, under Rule changes: approve or reject a driver's proposed change to a canon document (docs/schema.md, "Canon is written by proposal"). Approve previews the lines it adds and removes, with any that read as instructions to agents listed first; a proposal whose document changed since it was written can only be rejected, and a rejection needs the reason the proposer reads. Approve all, beside the group's heading, decides every pending proposal in one dialog: its preview lists each proposal with its planned outcome (the newest on each document approved unless stale, older ones rejected as superseded, stale ones as outdated), the flagged instruction lines first. Pending proposals are rows in Waiting for you.

Agents has three tabs. Roster lists live agents only. Archive (`?tab=archive`) lists the revoked ones: those the watcher pass has moved to `agents_archive`, with when and why, and those still waiting their 7 days, with the date they move (docs/auth.md). Claims is below. Both Roster and Archive read the feed's `agents`, the inventory `capsid_run_improve` action `status` serves.

**The automation switch** (the overnight run; ruled 2026-09-30) does not move when flipped. It opens a one-line reason beside it: Enter applies, Esc cancels and returns focus to the switch, and an empty reason is an error on the field that sends nothing. A reason is required in both directions, and the Worker refuses `overnight` without one. On Apply the app previews and performs in sequence, with no dialog; a refusal is shown beside the switch. Once it applies the switch moves, and the result stays in the message at the foot of the screen, with Undo, until it is dismissed or the next action replaces it (a result with a warning is never replaced; see below). Undo sends the reverse change as its own action with `undo: "true"` and the reason "Undo: " and the original reason, and the Worker writes `portal-undo-<action>` for it. Changing "Runs on" while the run is on is a change of mode and asks for a reason the same way; while it is off the choice is held until the switch turns on, starting at API.

Every other control opens a dialog that previews what will change and the audit rows it will write, and nothing happens until its perform button, named for the action ("Revoke agent", "Mark failed"), is pressed (the routes are below). For a one-way action (revoke, mark failed, release, close as shipped, remove a site, reset the breaker, approve or reject a canon proposal, approve all) the preview puts focus on Cancel. Esc and Cancel close the dialog; a click outside it does not, so a typed reason is not lost. Its result stays in the message until dismissed, and a warning that the click's audit row was not written is part of it. Refresh reads the feed again and runs one watcher pass on demand, at most once per two minutes.

**Warnings and failures stay until dismissed.** The message region at the foot of the screen holds a stack, newest first. A plain result is replaced by the next one. A result carrying a warning (an audit row naming you was not written, from a control, an Undo or a Refresh's `X-Capsid-Warning` header), an Undo that failed, and a failure (a Refresh, with the Worker's own reason; a copy; a sign out) each stay, whatever happens after them, until their own Dismiss. The missing audit row is the one fact Activity cannot show later, because the missing row is the failure. Only plain confirmations (Copied, the theme, a Refresh that worked or is rate limited) go to the toast that clears itself. For a blocked job the drawer also shows the command and the resume call, with Copy buttons. The command is shown only when its signature matches what the holder's block wrote; a changed one is withheld with a warning, and one written before blocks were signed is shown with an "Unsigned" note under it (`command_signature` in the feed, `src/job-signing.ts`).

## Needs you, the home

**Needs you** is the home page (`/portal/`), the answer to "what do I have to decide" (docs/design/design-portal-evaluation.md, DECIDE 1, ruled 2026-10-09; the Portal UX audit's B1, B3 and M3, 2026-10). It holds, in order:
- **Waiting for you**, every decision with its own buttons, so nothing needs a second page: the pull requests waiting for you (Approve, `approve_pr`, and Close, `pr_close`, with a box to tick each and "Approve N selected"), the questions and blocked jobs (Answer or Resume opens a reply box; Send is `resume_job` with the reply as its reason), the rule changes (Review, `canon_approve`, and Reject, `canon_reject`; "Approve all", `canon_approve_all`, or the ticked ones), the projects paused after repeated failures (Reset, `reset_breaker`), and then the rest of the inbox (`GET /ops/inbox`, src/inbox.ts, carried as `live.inbox`). Several ticked rows are one confirm that previews each control in turn and performs them one by one; a refused one is named and the rest still go. Empty is "Nothing waiting". The header's inbox count is this list's number, and no menu entry repeats it;
- **Problems**, critical then warnings, without what is already waiting above. Rows of one kind and cause fold into one row that opens in place to its first five. A critical row is never folded. Past eight rows the rest of the warnings wait behind "N more warnings". The watcher's attention-class findings (an agent out of its usual pattern) are warning rows marked "nothing suspended". One **Notices** row at the foot, closed, holds facts with nothing to do now: a site with no Cloudflare data, the Cloudflare read not configured, a check that could not run, CI that could not be read, site map drift, drivers silent for over 7 days. "Health not read" and "mirror not read" stay warnings, because an unknown backup is not a quiet fact;
- **Since you last looked**, one line, with its lists behind "Show them";
- **Weekly digest**, shown only while something is open: one block per site (namespace) listing the watcher's digest-class findings (CSP, security headers, analytics and beacon, unread pages, sha, weekly repair failures) with how long each has been open. They post no job. The same list is in the Monday email;
- **Trends**, folded: the charts load only when it is opened.

In chat, `capsid_admin_whats_next` (`src/whats-next.ts`, admin only, read-only) answers the same question in one read: blocked jobs asking a question, blocked jobs waiting on a command, stale jobs, every open pull request in every mapped repo with its CI verdict and whether it conflicts (marked `waiting_on_seat` when a driver's is green, not a draft and mergeable), and the watcher's checks that found something or could not run. A part it could not read is named in `not_checked`, never reported as nothing found.

Browser tests: dashboard/e2e/needs.spec.ts.

## The pages

The pages, grouped by task (docs/design/design-portal-evaluation.md section 2, DECIDE 2 and 8, ruled 2026-10-09; the Portal UX audit's sitemap, 2026-10). The words are the audit's; the internal ids (`queue`, `claims`, `namespace`) stay in the code and the addresses:
- **Now:** Needs you (above) and Health;
- **Work:** Pull requests and Jobs (`/portal/queue`);
- **Records:** Activity, Agents (its Agents, Archive and Checks tabs), Packages (its Published tab, while a package is configured, and its Shared tab) and Library;
- **Settings**, the menu's last entry, with a gear. It is also under the avatar.

Every menu entry has an icon, so the collapsed menu has no blank button. On a phone the tab bar is Needs you, Health and Pull requests, and the header's menu button lists every page. Every risky control (Mark failed, Remove, Revoke) sits alone in a red "Danger" area, never beside a safe one. The job drawer has one primary action for the job's state, the other safe ones under "More actions". The confirm dialog says what the action does in a sentence; the rows it changes and the audit rows it writes are behind "Details" (open from the start for a rule change, whose lines are the decision). On a phone no text is under 13 px.

Each old view's content moved whole, so nothing it showed was lost. An old address, such as a bookmark of `/portal/deploys`, is replaced by the page, tab or section that holds it now (`MOVED` in `dashboard/src/app/ctx.ts`), with no redirect period for the old letters (Dustin, 2026-10-09):

**Health** (`/portal/health`) answers "is anything broken" (the Portal UX audit's M1, m2 and m3). It holds, in order:
- one line: "All good", or how many problems there are and the first three by name, each a link to where it is fixed, then "N more" to the whole list on Needs you. They are the same problems as Needs you's, so the two pages never disagree;
- four tiles, each a link to its tab: Sites up, Open findings, Backup (the primary dump) and Mirror (the off-account copy), so a stale mirror never turns a good backup red;
- five tabs, one shown at a time, the choice in the address (`?tab=`): **Sites** (one row per site, with every column; only while a site is configured), **Problems** (the watcher's findings, the scheduled tasks and the daily maintenance pass's list), **Deploys**, **Backups** and **CI** (the default branches).

Its menu count is the number of critical problems, leaving out what waits on you. It has no Queue panel and no attention list. One namespace filter in the address (`?ns=`) narrows every tab and is kept when the tab changes. With no site configured there is no site tile and no Sites tab. The rules are the UI audit's (`capsid/research/audit-ui-patterns.md`, rulings 1 to 4 and 10). On Jobs, blocked jobs are ordered by priority, then the newest first, with those blocked for over 7 days after them; Done and Failed start closed. A queued job somebody began (`capsid_work_jobs` action `begin`, docs/work-queue.md) says who started it and how long ago. Parked jobs have their own group, each row saying when it comes back; a parked job stays in the feed whatever its age, so it never vanishes. A blocked job that asks a question is marked Question, its row says what it asks, and its drawer shows the question instead of a command. Stuck jobs appear in one place, the Stuck jobs panel (named "Stale jobs" before the UX audit), by the Worker's own rules (src/stale-jobs.ts): the list's old "Stale" group was folded into it (Dustin, 2026-10-09). The page's chart comes after its lists.

A page with three or more sections that is taller than two screens of the window gets an "On this page" bar of links to them (the Queue with a long list). A section inside another is not an entry.

## Its own host

The Portal is served at https://portal.dustinedwards.info, a Custom Domain on the capsid Worker ("one address pattern for the family"). It is a separate origin from every public site, so nothing on a public page can reach the Portal's session. `src/portal-host.ts` does it, first in the Worker's fetch (`src/index.ts`), before the OAuth provider, which answers its own routes (`/mcp`, `/token`, `/authorize`, its metadata) without reaching any other handler:

- **The Portal at the root.** On that host a request for `/x` is handled as `/portal/x`, so every Portal route, the gate and every check apply unchanged: `/` is the app, `/api/ops` the feed, `/callback` the sign-in's return.
- **Nothing else.** `/mcp`, `/health` and the `/ops` routes are on https://mcp.dustinedwards.info (`src/mcp-host.ts`). On the Portal host they are Portal paths behind the gate.
- **Old addresses.** A request for `/portal/...` on the Portal host is redirected (308, which keeps the method) to the same path at the root. The built app's hashed files are the exception: they keep their `/portal/assets/` paths on both hosts (the build's `base`), and the Worker serves them as they are.
- **One build, both hosts.** The app reads its base from its own address (`dashboard/src/lib/base.ts`): empty on the Portal host, and `/portal` on any other (the dev server and the test hosts). Its router and every API URL build on it.
- **The domain is managed in the dashboard.** The Custom Domain is added under the Worker's Domains in the Cloudflare dashboard. The deploy config declares no `routes`, so a deploy leaves it in place; a `routes` key added to the config would replace it on the next deploy (Cloudflare's Wrangler configuration docs, "Source of truth"). Another deployment of this repo names its own host in `PORTAL_HOST`.

## Where each view gets its data

The app reads one endpoint, `GET /portal/api/ops`, whose shape is `OpsFeed` in `src/ops-types/feed.ts`. The app and the Worker both typecheck against `src/ops-types/`. The feed has three parts.

**The snapshot** is the watcher's last pass, one KV read of `ops:snapshot`. The watcher writes it every 30 minutes, or on Refresh (`src/ops-snapshot.ts`, [autonomy.md](autonomy.md)). It holds:
- each check's result: clear, finding, or could not run;
- Capsid's `/health`;
- the backup mirror;
- each roster repo's latest CI run;
- the site configuration compared with the registered namespaces;
- a probe of every configured site, with a 7-day uptime ring;
- Cloudflare's view of each site, from `src/ops-cloudflare.ts`;
- each configured package as the pass read it, from `src/ops-packages.ts`.

**The live part** is read from D1 and KV on every request. It holds:
- every open job, and every job that ended in the last day;
- the agents and their records;
- pull requests from the last week;
- what auto-merge left for the seat;
- the site and package configuration;
- the D1 store's size, from the jobs read's `meta.size_after` (every D1 result carries it, so it costs no read of its own).

**The run ledger** is read from D1 on every request too: each scheduled task's five newest runs (below).

These read more when they open, and not on every poll:
- **Queue** reads `GET /portal/api/plan` for its Tonight section: the overnight plan over every namespace, the reader behind `capsid_work_jobs` action `list` view `plan` (docs/overnight.md), one group per repo with each planned job's estimate and whether it is gated, then what is skipped and why. A parked job is never planned.
- **Needs you** reads `GET /portal/api/digest?since=` for its "Since you last looked" strip, loaded after the page's first paint: what finished, what was merged, the pull requests ready for the seat and what blocked, the reader behind `capsid_work_jobs` action `list` view `digest`. `since` is when this browser last opened Needs you (localStorage `wf-last-looked`), or the last 24 hours with no record.
- **Jobs** reads `GET /portal/api/stale` for its Stuck jobs panel: the rows `capsid_work_jobs` action `list` with `stale: true` returns, over every namespace, each with the rule it met and why (docs/work-queue.md). It reads again with each feed, so a job resumed or failed from its drawer leaves the panel, and the Queue's namespace chips narrow it. A row opens the job's drawer, which holds the controls. The Queue's Jobs list leaves out every job the panel shows, so a stale job is listed once; until the panel has read, or if its first read fails, the list shows every job.
- **Health** reads `GET /portal/api/maintenance` for the Maintenance panel on its Problems tab, loaded after the page's first paint: the daily maintenance pass's last list (docs/work-queue.md), the broken things first, then what waits on the seat, tidying, what the pass did on its own, and what it could not read, with a line saying what it read. A row whose job is in the feed opens that job's drawer.
- **Settings**, in its Automation and namespaces section, reads `GET /portal/api/namespaces`: each namespace as `capsid_run_improve` action `status` reports it.
- **Activity** reads `GET /portal/api/activity`: the last 50 audit rows, filtered by namespace and actor. A job transition writes two rows with one action, actor and path, one for the job and one for its mirror document, and the view labels them `(job)` and `(mirror document)`. A row opens a drawer that reads that one row (`?id=`) and shows what it recorded: the reason typed with the change, a field-by-field before and after where the row carries both (the old value struck through above the new), and the row's other fields by name. The Worker turns the params into named fields (`src/audit-detail.ts`) and never sends them raw: a hash, a signature, a token or a nested value is counted as not shown and stays in the audit log.
- **Agents**, in its Checks tab, reads `GET /portal/api/claims` (and exports the aggregate on screen as three CSV files, by agent, waits and usage, under the filter in force; a cell a spreadsheet would read as a formula is prefixed with an apostrophe): what agents said beside what the Worker verified (below).
- **Packages** reads `GET /portal/api/packages/history` when a package's history is asked for (below).
- **Packages**, in its Shared tab, reads `GET /portal/api/shared-code` when it opens (below).
- **Packages**, in its Completeness tab, reads `GET /portal/api/completeness` when it opens (below).
- **Library** reads `GET /portal/api/library` when it opens, searches and opens a document (below).

## Scheduled tasks

Every scheduled task writes one row per run to `task_runs` (`migrations/0029_task_runs.sql`): which task, when it started and finished, its outcome, and one line on what it did or why it did not. The outcome is `ok`, `skipped`, `refused` or `threw`. Every task goes through `runTask` in `src/task-runs.ts`, the one writer:

- the three cron branches in `src/index.ts`: the backup, the skills refresh and the five-minute tick;
- the steps the tick carries (`src/tick.ts`), among them auto-merge, the watcher pass and the merge-state sweep.

A step that was not due makes no run and writes no row: the watcher between passes, the skills refresh on any day but its own, auto-merge under a disabled policy. A refused merge policy is a run, recorded as `refused`. A ledger write that fails never stops the task; it is logged as `TASK_RUN_UNRECORDED`, and the task then shows as quiet. Rows older than 14 days are pruned, 50 at a time, on each write.

Health's **Problems** tab shows every task in the Scheduled tasks panel: its state, its newest run and what it did, with the four runs before it folded underneath. A task is flagged, and listed in Problems, when its newest run threw or was refused (Failing), or when a periodic task has had no run in twice its period (Not running): the tick every 5 minutes, the watcher at its own cadence, the backup daily, the skills refresh weekly. A failing or quiet backup is critical; the rest are warnings. A task with no run recorded yet is shown as No run yet and is not flagged, since every task starts there after the migration. Adapted from Foxhound's cron run history.

## Packages

Optional, like the sites: with no package configured in Settings, the Portal's Packages page shows no Published tab and the watcher reads nothing for packages. Each watcher pass reads every configured npm package, each source failing on its own (`src/ops-packages.ts`, which cites each API's documentation):
- **Versions**: the registry's abbreviated document, `latest` and the other dist-tags.
- **Downloads**: the last 7 and 30 days from `api.npmjs.org`, and the last 7 days per version, the only per-version counts npm keeps. A package npm has not counted yet shows zero.
- **Dependents**: deps.dev's counts of packages that depend on the default version, directly or through another package. deps.dev calls them indicative, and no public API lists them, so the view links to npm's and deps.dev's lists. GitHub has no API for the repositories that use a package.
- **Repository**: stars, open issues (GitHub counts pull requests as issues; they are taken out), open pull requests (one page of 100; more shows as 100+) and the latest release, through the GitHub App. The first pass of each ISO week keeps these in `ops_package_weeks`.

A pass costs up to nine requests per package. No rate limit is published for npm or deps.dev.

**The daily history** is read when asked for, not pass by pass: from each name's first publish (the full registry document's `time.created`) to yesterday, in ranges of at most 540 days, under npm's 18-month cap, since npm silently shortens a longer range. A package's former name (`formerly`, as enarratio was abscissa) is read the same way and shown joined to it, each day labeled. It is cached six hours in KV (`packages:history:v1:<name>`), about 30 bytes a day. A range npm would not answer is named as missing, never counted as zero.

**Exact times** in a panel or a detail field are shown in the viewer's own time zone with UTC beside it, in a `<time>` element, and need no hover. Rows keep relative times.

**Relative times** ("2m ago") are measured on the server's clock: the app takes the skew between its clock and the feed's `generated` time when each feed arrives, and never measures a row against a time earlier than the read that returned it. A timestamp with no zone is read as UTC, since every time the Worker writes is.

## Library

The knowledge store, read-only. Writing stays in chat and with drivers, so the page has one button, Search. `GET /portal/api/library` (`src/portal-library.ts`) answers one of three ways, by its query string:

- **No argument:** the shelves, over every namespace. Decisions are documents of type `decision`; Rulings are paths under `rulings/`; Research is paths under `research/`. Nothing under `archive/` is shelved. One statement ordered by namespace and path, so it walks the unique index and stops at 500 rows; past that the page says to search.
- **`?q=`:** full text search over every namespace, the best 25 by bm25, with the matched words marked. A query FTS5 refuses (a hyphen, a quote) is retried as a phrase.
- **`?ns=&path=`:** one document, its text as written, and the typed edges declared on it and pointing at it. Every job id in the text opens that job's drawer over the page.

Search, the document read and the edges go through `src/doc-reads.ts`, the same readers `capsid_find_docs` action `search` and `capsid_read_doc` actions `read` and `links` use, so the Library and chat cannot disagree. The route writes nothing, not even the conventions-read row (that row records an agent loading the rules). The page's own state is in its address: `?q=` for a search and `?doc=<namespace>/<path>` for a document, so either can be reloaded or shared. Tests: test-integration/portal-library.test.ts.

## Trends

`GET /portal/api/trends?weeks=` (`src/trends.ts`, `src/portal-trends.ts`; DECIDE 5 of docs/design/design-portal-evaluation.md, ruled 2026-10-09) serves the series the charts need and the feed does not carry. Admin only (portalGate), read-only. `weeks` is 1 to 26, default 8; every time is UTC and weeks start on Monday.

| Series | Chart | Read from |
|---|---|---|
| `minutes`: per ISO week, minutes, sessions and decisions | 1, minutes of Dustin per week | `audit_log` rows by an `access:` actor and human `job_touches`, split into sessions at a 5-minute gap, each its span plus 1 minute. Decisions are human touches other than notes, plus approvals by an `access:` actor. An estimate, and it says so: reading the Portal writes nothing, so it is not counted |
| `queue`: per day for 14 days, posted, ended, open at the day's end | 3, is the queue growing or draining | `jobs`: created, ended (done, failed, superseded; such a job never moves again, so its last update is when), and the open count when the window starts |
| `cost`: per week, model spend and the outcomes that carried one | 6, what the work costs | `job_outcomes.cost_usd` (the session telemetry total, written when a job ends) summed by `recorded_at`. A week with no costed outcome is null, not 0 |
| `merges`: per week, auto, approved, seat, closed; and each rollback | 7, how much merges with no human | `audit_log` actions `auto-merged`, `approved-merged`, `manage_pr` (a merge or a close) and `pr-closed`; `deploy_outcomes` rolled back or failed to roll back. A policy merge that also closed an approval writes both `auto-merged` and `approved-merged`; it is counted once, as auto |
| `backups`: 14 daily, 8 weekly and 6 monthly slots, filled or not | 10, is every retention slot filled | the complete runs (`_complete.json`) under `backups/json/` in the backup bucket |
| `ci`: per roster repo and day for 14 days, green, red or none | 11, which repos are red, and for how long | GitHub, each repo's default branch runs; a day is red when any workflow's last completed run up to then failed and green when every one passed, carried over a day with none. Kept in KV for an hour (`portal:trends-ci:v1`) only when every repo was read; a repo that could not be read is in `not_read`, never drawn green |

## Sites are configuration

Which sites the Portal watches is configuration each install edits, not code (ruled 2026-09-29: monitoring panels are optional and configured per install). The rows live in the D1 table `ops_sites` (`migrations/0022_ops_sites.sql`, `src/ops-sites.ts`) and are edited in the **Settings** view.

- **A row per namespace.** A row with an origin is a site: its name, its origin (`https://` and a hostname, nothing else), an optional health route, its platform (`cloudflare` or `vercel`), and optionally the Worker script that serves it. A row with no origin records that the namespace serves no site, so its absence from the probes is a decision.
- **Edits are controls.** Add, edit and remove go through the same preview and perform as every other control, as `site_add`, `site_edit` and `site_remove`. The preview names every field that changes. An edit or a removal carries the row's `revision` from the preview and is refused if the row changed since. Each writes `ops-site-added`, `ops-site-edited` or `ops-site-removed` (with the row before and after) and then the click row.
- **What is checked.** The origin must be `https://` and a lowercase public hostname: no user, port, path, query or fragment, no IP address, no single-label or local name. A health path starts with `/` and uses only letters, digits and `- . _ ~ /`, with no empty, `.` or `..` segment. A script is named only for a Cloudflare site. A new row's namespace must be registered. The table's own CHECK constraints hold the origin and platform to both or neither.
- **Nothing configured.** With no row that has an origin, the watcher probes nothing and reads nothing from Cloudflare, and the Portal hides Health's Sites tab and its site tile. Settings stays, since that is where the first site is added.
- **Every pass reads it.** The watcher reads the table at the start of each pass. If the read fails, no site check runs that pass: nothing is probed on a guess and no open site finding is cleared.
- **A check on demand.** `capsid_inspect_repo` action `site` (`src/site-check.ts`) reads one namespace's site from its row, on its own origin only: the health route as the watcher probes it, one page (its status and the security headers), and one path that must refuse a caller with no credential (the site's operator API unless another is named). It returns statuses and verdicts, never body text, and a namespace with no row is told the admin adds one with `capsid_admin_run_controls` control `site_add`.
- **The site map check** compares the rows with the registered namespaces both ways. A registered namespace with no row, or a row for a namespace that is not registered, is a watcher finding.
- **The repo map check** (`src/unmapped-repos.ts`) lists every repo the GitHub App's installations can see and compares it with the namespace mapping. Each repo no namespace maps is one watcher finding (`unmapped-repo-<owner>--<name>`) naming the repo, its visibility, whether it is archived, its last push, and the namespace its name suggests. It never maps: mapping is the authorization boundary and stays with the seat (`capsid_admin_manage_namespaces` action `update`). A repo that is unmapped on purpose goes in `capsid/unmapped-repos.md`, one `owner/name` per line (`#` starts a comment); the document can only silence a finding. The listing uses a token minted for the call with the `metadata:read` permission only, held in memory and never stored. A list that cannot be read whole, lists no repos, or sits beside a corrupt mapping is the finding `unmapped-repos-unreadable`, and the check does not count as run, so an open finding is not cleared on no evidence. Every finding states how many repos were read and how many are mapped.
- **The seed** is the list the code held before the move, so nothing changed on the deploy that moved it. Capsid's own row was probed in-process rather than over HTTP until `0052_ops_sites_hosts.sql` moved it to https://mcp.dustinedwards.info and off that probe, so it is read from the network like every site; the Portal cannot set the in-process probe, and an edit that changes Capsid's origin clears it. A row keyed `<namespace>-<host>` (capsid-portal, foxhound-admin-mcp) is a further host of that namespace: probed and alerted like a site, not reported as an unregistered namespace.

## Site repairs

A site may let Capsid call an allowlist of its own repair tools (job_09e5f6cbf782), so its convergence view, its repair buttons and its watchdog can live in Capsid. A site opts in by configuration: the `operator` column of its `ops_sites` row (`migrations/0035_ops_sites_operator.sql`, `src/site-operator.ts`), a JSON object of `path` (the operator route on its origin), `auth_var` (the name of the Capsid Worker secret that holds its operator token, which must end in `_OPERATOR_TOKEN`), `repairs` (health check name to repair tool, in repair order), `weekly` (tools the watcher runs once a week) and `secrets` (the names of the site Worker's secrets to report). The migration opts dustinedwards in with its own `app/lib/health/repair.mjs` mapping, less `media-backup-drift`. `site_edit` from the `capsid_admin_run_controls` tool sets it (`operator`, or `none` to clear it); the Settings form does not show it, and an edit that does not name it leaves it.

- **Two allowlists, both in the Worker, checked before every request.** The ceiling, in code: a repair is a `sync_*` tool other than `sync_status`, or `refresh_citations`. `backup_media`, and every write tool the site's operator API serves, is never called. Then the site's own list: the tools its `repairs` and `weekly` name. A configuration that breaks the ceiling is refused whole when it is saved, and a stored one is checked again before each call. `test/site-operator.test.ts` ("PLANT: backup_media and an unknown tool are refused at the Worker before any request") and `test-integration/site-repair.test.ts` ("PLANT: refuses backup_media and a tool outside the site's list") hold it.
- **The token.** Read by name from the Worker's environment, sent only to the site's own origin, never logged, returned or put in an audit row. Unset, nothing is called and the Portal and the incident say so. Capsid never merges and never mints.
- **Run repair** is the registry control `site_repair` (`namespace`, `tool`), from Health's Sites tab or the `capsid_admin_run_controls` tool, previewed and performed like every control. Each call that leaves the Worker writes an `audit_log` row (`site-repair`, with the tool, the surface, the HTTP status and the site's read-back verdict) and a `task_runs` row (`site-repair`).
- **The rate limit** (`src/site-repair.ts`), per site and tool: any call waits 2 minutes after the last; the watcher waits 30 minutes and makes at most 4 calls a day, after which only a person can run it and the incident says so.
- **Site sync** (the convergence view, `GET /portal/api/convergence`, Health's Sites tab): per opted-in site, its operator API's `sync_status` and its health route's checks, read live, each failing check with the repair it maps to and a Run button, the weekly tools, the last repairs, and the site Worker's secrets by name, set or not. The names come from the watcher's pass (`GET /accounts/{id}/workers/scripts/{script}/secrets` with `CF_OPS_TOKEN`, permission **Account / Workers Scripts / Read**, the one the deploys column already needs), because no Portal request calls Cloudflare. A value is never read: Cloudflare's list has none.
- **The watcher** (`src/site-watch.ts`), on each pass: reads the health route; when every failing check maps to an allowlisted repair it runs them in order and reads the health again; when a failing check maps to nothing, it repairs nothing, because a failure with an unmapped part may share its cause. Still unhealthy, or a repair failed: the finding `site-health-<namespace>` opens under Health's Problems and clears when a pass finds the site healthy. A weekly tool runs once every 7 days; a failure is `site-weekly-<namespace>-<tool>`. A change between healthy and not mails once, from `ALERT_FROM` to `ALERT_EMAIL` through a `send_email` binding named `EMAIL`, where the seat has set them; without them the finding still opens and the log says the mail was not sent.

## Site keys

A site's operator key is the site API's key (DrDustinEdwards/site-api): the site's Worker holds it as `CARREL_SITE_KEY`, and Carrel holds the same value as `SITE_<NAMESPACE>_KEY` (upper case, dashes as underscores) to call that site. `capsid_admin_set_site_key` (`src/site-key.ts`), a Capsid Admin tool, mints a new one and sets it on both sides through the Cloudflare API. It is admin only, and from an OAuth session it needs a recent sign-in or step-up action (2 hours sliding, 12 hours at most; docs/auth.md, the Capsid Admin endpoints and their step-up).

- **The value is never shown.** It is not returned, logged, written to the audit row or put in an error. It exists in that call's memory and in the two secrets.
- **The token.** `CF_SECRETS_TOKEN`, a Cloudflare API token apart from `CF_OPS_TOKEN` because it can write: **Account / Workers Scripts / Edit**. The account is `CF_ACCOUNT_ID`, or `R2_ACCOUNT_ID` where that is unset. Unset, nothing is minted, and a 403 from Cloudflare says the token needs that permission.
- **The site's Worker** is the script on the namespace's `ops_sites` row. A row that names no Cloudflare script is refused before a key is minted.
- **The order.** The site's Worker first, then Carrel. When the site refuses, nothing changed and Carrel is not touched. When the site takes the key and Carrel does not, the answer says the two sides differ and Carrel's calls to the site are refused until they match; calling again mints a fresh key and sets both. Nothing is retried silently.
- **The record.** One `audit_log` row, `set_site_key`, with each side's script, secret name and whether it was set, never the value.

## No data is a state, never a zero

A value the feed does not have is shown as **No data** with its reason. It is never a zero, a dash that reads as zero, or a green default.

- **Deploys and errors.** These come from Cloudflare and need `CF_OPS_TOKEN` (below). Until it is set, both columns say "Cloudflare read not configured".
  - A Vercel site says its platform is not Cloudflare.
  - A custom-domain site whose Worker the watcher cannot find says so. Script names are never guessed: a `*.workers.dev` host names its script, and any other host is resolved from the account's Workers custom domains.
  - If the analytics query fails, the error column shows the query's error text.
- **Uptime.** Each half-hour slot the watcher did not run is hatched, not counted as up.
- **Backups.** Capsid's backup age comes from its own `/health`. No other site reports a backup yet: that arrives with each site's `/health` contract, one PR in each site's repo. Until then the column says so.
- **The store's size.** Health's Backups tab, in its primary panel, shows the D1 store's size against the per-database cap, 10 GB on Workers Paid (`D1_CAP_BYTES` in `src/ops-feed.ts`; 500 MB on Workers Free, the one line to change). From half the cap it is flagged there and listed in Problems as a warning, Foxhound's threshold. The size is read in the admin-only feed, not in the public `/health`. A size D1 did not report reads "Not reported", never zero.

## The Cloudflare token

`CF_OPS_TOKEN` is a Cloudflare API token that can only read. The watcher needs two permissions:
- **Account Analytics Read**, for requests and errors per Worker per hour;
- **Workers Scripts Read**, for deployments, the custom-domain list and the secret names of a site that opted into repairs (names only; above, "Site repairs").

The watcher's live checks need one more for the `analytics` rule (docs/live-checks.md):
- **Account / Account Settings / Read**, for the Web Analytics sites list (`GET /accounts/{id}/rum/site_info/list`). Add it to the same token the same way; until it is there the rule files `live-analytics-unread-<site>`.

The certificate-expiry read ("Phone alerts", item 8) needs one more:
- **Zone / SSL and Certificates / Read**, for each zone's certificate packs (`GET /zones/{id}/ssl/certificate_packs`), with **Zone / Zone / Read** to find the zones. Until it is there the read fails per host and no expiry alert can fire.

The admin-only `capsid_admin_read_ops` MCP tool's Cloudflare configuration actions (`src/tools/cloudflare.ts`, reading through `src/ops-cloudflare-config.ts`) need four more:
- **Account / Access: Apps and Policies / Read**, for `access_apps` and `access_policies`;
- **Account / Email Routing Addresses / Read**, for `email_addresses`;
- **Zone / Zone / Read**, to list the zones `email_rules` reads;
- **Zone / Email Routing Rules / Read**, for `email_rules`.

A 403 from any of these names the permission that is missing. The tool copies named fields only, so an Access for SaaS client secret, a SCIM credential or an application's aud tag never reaches the caller. It runs once per tool call, never from a Portal page.

Its `builds` and `build_logs` actions (`src/workers-builds.ts`) need one more:
- **Account / Workers CI / Read**, for each Worker's Workers Builds and one build's log. Add it the same way; until it is there both actions refuse with a 403 that names it, never an empty list.

To add the four to the existing token:
1. Open dash.cloudflare.com/profile/api-tokens.
2. On the token **capsid-portal-read**, choose **Edit**.
3. Add **Account / Access: Apps and Policies / Read**, **Account / Email Routing Addresses / Read**, **Zone / Email Routing Rules / Read** and **Zone / Zone / Read**.
4. Under **Zone Resources**, choose **All zones from the account**.
5. Choose **Continue to summary**, then **Update token**.

The secret value does not change, so no Worker secret is updated.

The account id comes from `CF_ACCOUNT_ID`, or from `R2_ACCOUNT_ID` when that is unset (the same account). Both are Worker secrets, never in the repo. The watcher calls Cloudflare once per pass and never once per page load.

## The Access sync token

`POST /ops/access-sync` (`src/access-sync.ts`) is how an app's members page reaches Cloudflare without holding a token (Dustin, 2026-10-09: Capsid is the only holder). The app posts its whole current members list with its own minted agent key, which must hold the write grant on its namespace. Capsid writes that namespace's pinned Access group so it holds exactly those emails, then frees the Zero Trust seats of the people the app says it removed who the group held before the write (a removed email the group never held is reported in `seats.not_members` and its seat left alone). It never touches a policy, so an admin policy that lists Dustin's emails directly cannot be changed by a sync.

The write token is its own secret, `CF_ACCESS_SYNC_TOKEN`, never the read-only `CF_OPS_TOKEN`. Create it once, at dash.cloudflare.com/profile/api-tokens, **Create Token**, **Create Custom Token**, named **capsid-access-sync**, with exactly these three permissions:
1. **Account / Access: Groups / Write**, to read and write rule groups. It is narrower than "Access: Organizations, Identity Providers, and Groups Write", which would also reach organization settings and identity providers.
2. **Account / Access: Users / Read**, to find a removed person's seat.
3. **Account / Zero Trust: Seats / Write**, to free that seat.

Under **Account Resources**, choose this one account. Then **Continue to summary**, **Create Token**, and `npx wrangler secret put CF_ACCESS_SYNC_TOKEN` with the value. The value is shown once and is never pasted anywhere else. A 403 names the permission that is missing.

Each app's group is pinned by hand in `access_sync_groups` (`migrations/0040_access_sync_groups.sql`): the namespace, the group's id and its name. The caller never names a group. Before every write the group is read, and the sync refuses with a 409 and writes nothing when the name differs from the pin, or when the group holds an include rule that is not a single email (a rule the sync would erase). A Cloudflare refusal answers 502, so the app shows "Waiting for Cloudflare" and retries. Every call, refused or not, is one `audit_log` row with action `access-sync`.

## Phone alerts

Settings has a **Phone alerts** panel (`dashboard/src/views/PhoneAlerts.tsx`, loaded on its own). **Turn on for this device** asks the browser for notification permission, registers the Portal's service worker (`dashboard/public/sw.js`, which shows an alert and opens its item on a tap, and caches nothing), subscribes with the VAPID public key and stores the subscription in `push_subscriptions` (`migrations/0041_push_subscriptions.sql`). Each device can be turned off from the list, and **Send a test alert** sends one alert to every device and shows each push service's answer.

The five-minute tick's `push` step (`src/push.ts`) sends an alert for eight things only (docs/design/design-merge-pipeline.md section 5; Dustin, 2026-10-09, DECIDE 5 and 6; the sixth from job_0368576da167; the seventh and eighth from area 8, 2026-10-30 and 2026-10-31):
1. a `typed` pull request (a money path) waiting more than 2 hours, counted from the first tick that saw it typed;
2. a rollback or a failed rollback recorded in `deploy_outcomes` in the last day;
3. a job blocked at the correction cap in the last day;
4. a question over 4 hours old on a job with priority 70 or more;
5. auto-merge paused for a namespace by the safety shutoff (docs/autonomy.md, "The safety meter, the shutoff, and existing tests"), sent once per pause.
6. a pull request labeled `visual` waiting for Dustin's look (src/visual-pr.ts), sent once per head, so a new push asks again. The Portal's **Waiting for you** row for it shows the preview link and screenshots its body names, and its Approve button merges it on green CI.
7. a site, from the tick's `site-pulse` step (`src/site-pulse.ts`), which probes every site's health route every five minutes the way the watcher does and keeps its state in KV `site-pulse:state`: down on two probes in a row (once per outage, naming the reason), back up after that (once), and degraded, its health body reporting warn, `ok: false` or a backup warning (once a UTC day while it lasts, naming the failing checks or the warning). These open Health's Sites tab.
8. a TLS certificate on a watched host that expires within 14 days, and again within 3 (`src/tls-expiry.ts`). The watcher pass reads each Cloudflare zone's certificate packs beside the domain check and keeps the soonest expiry per host in KV `tls:expiry:v1`; the push step reads only that. It needs **Zone / SSL and Certificates / Read** on `CF_OPS_TOKEN` ("The Cloudflare token", below); a host it cannot read keeps its last date.

Each item is sent once (KV `push:state` keeps what was sent for 30 days), at most three items a tick, and an item no device received is tried again on the next tick. An alert names the repo and the pull request number, or the namespace and job id, and its class. It never carries a title and never an Approve button: a typed pull request is approved only by typing its number, a visual one after looking at it in the Portal, and the other six are not pull requests, so there is no approve-from-push route. Every alert opens its own item on https://portal.dustinedwards.info (the UX audit's M2, 2026-10): a pull request opens its card on Pull requests (`/prs#pr-<repo>-<number>`), a rollback opens Health's Deploys tab, a job opens its drawer on Jobs (`/queue/job/<id>`), a pause opens Auto-merge on Pull requests (`/prs#auto-merge`), and the test alert opens the home page. A tap moves a Portal tab already open to that page and brings it forward, and opens a new one only when none is open. The service worker opens only a page on its own scope; any other url opens the Portal itself. The Worker encrypts each alert to the browser's own keys (RFC 8291) and signs the request with the VAPID key (RFC 8292), with WebCrypto only (`src/web-push.ts`), so the push service (Google's, for Chrome on Android) sees neither the text nor a third-party account. A subscription whose endpoint is not on a known push service is refused before it is stored, and one the push service answers 404 or 410 for is marked `gone`. Audit rows: `push-device-on`, `push-device-off`, `push-test`, `push-sent` and `push-device-gone`.

To set it up, the seat, one step at a time:
1. Apply the migration: `npx wrangler d1 migrations apply capsid --remote`. It is additive: one table, one index.
2. Make and store the key: `node scripts/vapid-keys.mjs`. It puts `VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY` with wrangler and prints only the public half. Running it again replaces the key, and every device must turn alerts on again.
3. Deploy, open Settings on the phone, and press **Turn on for this device**, then **Send a test alert**.

Until the key is set the panel says so. With no key, or no device on, the `push` step mails the same urgent items to `ALERT_EMAIL` instead (the daily digest's `EMAIL` binding and its settings, "Daily digest" below), each once, audited `push-emailed`; with the digest's mail off too it sends nothing and says why.

## Daily digest

Everything that is not urgent enough for a phone alert waits for one email a day (`src/digest.ts`; docs/design/design-merge-pipeline.md section 5, DECIDE 5). Once a UTC day after 12:00 the tick's `digest` step mails `ALERT_EMAIL`: the pull requests waiting on Dustin by class (a typed one says to approve it by typing its number), questions, blocked jobs, rollbacks, and what finished or became ready since the last digest that was sent. On Mondays UTC it also carries a **Weekly digest** section: the watcher's open digest-class findings per site (`src/watcher-digest.ts`), the same list the Portal shows. It reads the same reader as `capsid_work_jobs` action `list` view `digest`, so the mail and the Portal agree. A send Cloudflare refuses is recorded for the day (KV `digest:last`) and shown in Settings, and the next day's digest covers both days.

**It does nothing until Email Routing is on.** Cloudflare sends from a Worker only from a domain with Email Routing turned on and only to a verified destination address, and turning it on is an account setting only Dustin can change. So the digest needs the `send_email` binding `EMAIL`, `ALERT_EMAIL`, `ALERT_FROM` and the var `EMAIL_ROUTING` set to `"on"`. Until all four are set, every tick returns at once, and Settings, **Daily digest**, says which is missing.

Turning it on, one step at a time:
1. **Dustin:** at dash.cloudflare.com open the dustinedwards.info zone, then **Email**, **Email Routing**. If it says the domain already receives mail through another provider (existing MX records), stop and tell the seat: turning Email Routing on would move that mail.
2. **Dustin:** press **Get started** (or **Enable Email Routing**), then **Add records and enable**. Cloudflare adds the MX and SPF records itself.
3. **Dustin:** on **Destination addresses**, **Add destination address**: the address the digest should go to.
4. **Dustin:** open the verification email Cloudflare sends there and press **Verify email address**. The address shows **Verified**.

## Building and changing it

`dashboard/` is its own package (React 19, Vite, wouter), with its own lockfile and README.
- **Develop:** `npm --prefix dashboard run dev` serves it with fake sample data (`dashboard/dev/sample-feed.json`, checked against `OpsFeed`).
- **Design tokens:** every color, font and shadow is a custom property from Capsomer, the shared design system (`capsomer` in `dashboard/package.json`, pinned to a release tag; `main.tsx` imports `capsomer/tokens.css`). Capsomer's `tokens/palette.mjs` generates the colors and its own `npm run check` refuses any text or control pair below WCAG 2.2's bar (4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for a boundary) in either theme. Capsomer v0.9.0, the pinned release, ships the purple family palette that arrived in v0.3.0 (accent `#8c5fd2`, with `--accent-text` for text, links in `--accent-text`) and replaced the Portal's own seed `#4F2D7F` of 2026-09-30; neutrals and statuses moved a few hex steps and every pair still passes the contrast check. The Portal is excluded from Cloudflare Web Analytics and its page CSP stays strict: it is a private tool, so it stays out of analytics and the CSP names no analytics host. Capsomer's rules sit in `cap.*` layers, so the Portal's own unlayered styles win. Change a color in Capsomer, never here.
- **Deploy:** `scripts/deploy.mjs` builds it into `dashboard/dist` before every deploy, and the Worker serves those files as its static assets. A failed build stops the deploy.
- **Browser tests:** `npm run test:browser`, after `npm run build:dashboard`, drives the built app in Chromium through Playwright (`dashboard/e2e`). It runs under `vite preview` with the dev mock and the Worker's own page CSP (`src/dashboard-csp.ts`). CI runs it after the build. What it covers:
  - Needs you, the home: each row's decision (approve, close, answer, resume, a rule change, a reset) previews the right control and performs it;
  - the confirm dialog: every Preview ends in a preview, a refusal, a timeout or a stated reason;
  - the automation switches: no move before the reason is applied, the empty-reason error, Esc, a refusal beside the switch, and the message with Undo that stays;
  - a canon proposal's review, a site repair from the convergence panel, the site configuration's add, edit and remove, sign-out, and a blocked command's signature;
  - no sideways scrolling at 1024 px on every view and in each drawer, and on a phone.
- **Size budget:** CI and every deploy run `dashboard/scripts/size-budget.mjs`, which fails closed. The initial JavaScript must be at most 119 KB gzip, each lazily loaded view at most 40 KB, the CSS loaded with the entry at most 19 KB, and the CSS loaded with later chunks (the dialogs and the command menu) at most 12 KB together. The script's header records each raise and why.
- **Assets config:** the `assets` block in `wrangler.jsonc.example` must keep `run_worker_first: true` and `not_found_handling: "none"`, and `test/dry-run-config.test.ts` fails if either changes. Without them the platform could answer a browser's `/authorize` or `/portal/callback` with the app's `index.html`, or serve the app's files without the gate.

## Who gets in

The administrator's Access session, or a co-owner's, and nothing else (`src/portal-auth.ts`).

- **The sign-in.** The Portal signs in through the same Cloudflare Access for SaaS app and the same `ADMIN_EMAIL` or `CO_OWNER_EMAILS` check as the MCP flow ([auth.md](auth.md)), with its own callback at `/portal/callback`, and turns the verified email into a signed cookie, `capsid_portal`, that lasts 24 hours, the approved admin session (Dustin, 2026-10-31, NIST SP 800-63B-4). An hour with no request ends it: site-runtime's `adminIdle` times the session, as it times the sites' Access sessions (`withPortalIdle` in `src/portal-auth.ts`, its last-request cookie `__Host-admin-seen`), and the browser is sent to Access's logout, so the next visit asks for a sign-in. The check runs again on every request. Each sign-in, refused sign-in, sign-out and idle end is one auth event line in the Worker's log (site-runtime `./log`, OWASP names, the address as a digest).
- **Co-owner.** A co-owner is admitted exactly like the administrator: her email in the optional secret `CO_OWNER_EMAILS` (comma separated; entries trimmed, exact match, no case folding), checked on the sign-in and on every request. Her clicks are recorded as `access:<her email>`, the email Access verified, and the Portal shows her as signed in under it. The step-up applies to her as to the administrator, and the push alerts' VAPID subject stays `ADMIN_EMAIL`. She cannot change who is admitted: `ADMIN_EMAIL` and `CO_OWNER_EMAILS` are set only in Cloudflare, so she cannot remove or demote the primary owner from inside Capsid. To add her, set `CO_OWNER_EMAILS` as a Secret (Type: Secret, not Text) in the Cloudflare dashboard, Workers & Pages, capsid, Settings, Variables and Secrets. The Access policies that admit the admin must list her email too.
- **Cookies.** `capsid_portal`, `capsid_portal_csrf` and the sign-in's `capsid_portal_state` are all `HttpOnly; Secure; SameSite=Lax` and **host-only: none ever carries a `Domain` attribute**, so no other subdomain of dustinedwards.info, the public sites included, can read or set them. On portal.dustinedwards.info, a host that serves nothing but the Portal, they are `Path=/`; on workers.dev they are `Path=/portal`, so `/mcp` and `/ops` there never see them. The browser enforces the scope too: a `Path=/` cookie is named `__Host-` and a `/portal` one `__Secure-` (`cookieName` in `src/auth.ts`). For one release a request's bare old name is still read and sign-out clears it, so a session from before the rename keeps working until it expires. `test/portal-login.test.ts` fails if any of them gains a `Domain` or leaves its host's path. The sign-in returns only to a Portal path; anything else stored as the return lands on the Portal's root.
- **Sign out** (`POST /portal/api/sign-out`, the button in the top bar) expires `capsid_portal` and `capsid_portal_csrf` in this browser. It passes the same checks as an action, so another site cannot sign the administrator out. It also ends the session on the server: the cookie's random session id is written to D1 (`portal_session_ends`, migrations/0055), and a cookie carrying it is refused, so a copy taken from this browser ends too, at once, while the person's sessions in other browsers go on. The idle end does the same. The Access session at the team domain is Cloudflare's, so after a sign-out the next visit may sign in again without asking for the email.
- **Machines.** An operator key or an agent key gets a 403 that says so. Those authenticate to `/ops/mcp` (and the admin's key to `/ops/mcp/admin`), which serve the same state through `capsid_run_improve` action `status` and `capsid_work_jobs`. A login redirect would send a machine to the Access sign-in.
- **It never merges and it never mints.** Merging can start a CI deploy, so that stays with `capsid_close_pr` action `merge` behind a caller holding `can_merge`. Minting hands out a key, so that stays with the `capsid_admin_manage_agents` tool. Neither is in the Portal's action list, and a test asserts their absence.
- **Each click names where it came from.** A Portal click's audit row (`portal-<action>`, `portal-undo-<action>`) and a Refresh's `portal-ops-refresh` row carry `source_address`: the address the request came from, Cloudflare's `CF-Connecting-IP`, which the edge sets and a client cannot. A value that is not an IPv4 or IPv6 address is recorded as null, and so is a request with none. The Activity drawer shows it as Source address.

## Routes

Every route but the callback answers to one gate, `portalGate`: the Access session and the `ADMIN_EMAIL` or `CO_OWNER_EMAILS` check on every request, a 403 for any `Authorization` header, and the sign-in for no session. The router matches the callback and every `/portal/api/` route before the app's catch-all.

- **`GET /portal/api/ops`** returns `OpsFeed` (`src/ops-types/feed.ts`), uncached: the watcher's last pass from KV, plus open jobs and jobs that ended in the last day, the agents with their records, pull requests recorded in the last week, the awaiting-seat set, and `live.findings`, the watcher's open digest and attention findings (those that post no job). It also carries the `csrf` value the controls send back, and `user` (name and initials) for the top bar avatar: the `name` claim of the Access ID token (the profile scope sends it when the identity provider has one), else the local part of the email, read from the signed session cookie and never from configuration (`portalDisplay` in `src/portal-auth.ts`; `test/ops-feed.test.ts`). One request costs 14 D1 statements plus one per scheduled task and 10 KV reads, run concurrently (one of the KV reads the auto-merge safety report, `live.merge_safety`, which the CI view shows as a table per namespace; one of the D1 statements the signed policy's read, `live.merge_policy`, which the Auto-merge policy panel shows). `src/ops-feed.ts` states them and an integration test counts them.
- **`POST /portal/api/ops/refresh`** runs one watcher pass now and returns the new feed. It needs the header `X-Capsid-Ops: refresh`, which a cross-site form cannot send, and it runs at most once per two minutes (KV `ops:refresh:last`; a 429 with `Retry-After` inside that window, and a 503 when the stamp cannot be read or written). The click is audited as `portal-ops-refresh` under `access:<email>`.
- **`POST /portal/api/actions/preview`** and **`POST /portal/api/actions/perform`** are the controls' two doors in the Portal (`src/portal-actions.ts`; the controls themselves, their registry and the confirmation are in `src/controls.ts`, and the admin-only `capsid_admin_run_controls` MCP tool is the same registry's other door, with the same preview and perform and its own `control-<action>` audit rows). The confirm is a second request, as ruled 2026-09-11:
  - The preview takes `{action, params}`, reads the current state, writes nothing, and returns what will change, the audit rows the perform will write, and a signed token. The token is good for five minutes and carries the action, its params, the admin's email and a one-time id.
  - The perform takes only `{token}`, so what runs is exactly what the dialog showed.
  - The Portal cannot merge a pull request or mint a credential: `test/portal-actions.test.ts` asserts that `merge`, `manage_pr`, `mint`, `mint_operator_key` and `mint_agent` are refused as actions.
  - A confirmation is used once (2026-10-06, the controls ruling D2): the perform spends the token's id in KV (`control-nonce:<id>`, kept until the token would have expired) before the control runs, so a second perform of the same token, from the Portal or from the `capsid_admin_run_controls` tool, is refused with 409 and writes nothing. Until then a replay inside the five minutes was allowed, because every transition is guarded on the state it moves from; that guard is still what stops two performs in the same instant, since KV has no compare-and-set. A token is spent even when the control then refuses it: preview again.
  - Both refuse a `Sec-Fetch-Site` that is present and not `same-origin`.
  - Both need the header `X-Capsid-CSRF` to equal the cookie `capsid_portal_csrf`. The feed body carries that value, and a cross-site page cannot read the feed.
  - Refusals: 400 refused, 403 CSRF or cross-site, 409 already used (preview again), 410 expired (preview again), 413 too large.
  - A perform writes the shared mutator's audit row, then `portal-<action>` under `access:<email>`, with `surface: "portal"` in its params (`control-<action>` and `"chat"` from the `capsid_admin_run_controls` tool). Since 2026-10-06 the rows `capsid_admin_control_improve` writes for a caller name that caller, not `improve-loop`; the improve switches' rows (`improve-mode-set`, `improve-paused`, `improve-unpaused`) stay in the log from before the loop was removed. Rows from before the move say `console-<action>` ([schema.md](schema.md)).
- **`GET /portal/api/namespaces`** returns each roster namespace as `capsid_run_improve` action `status` reports it, from the same function. **`GET /portal/api/activity?namespace=&actor=`** returns the last 50 audit rows, filtered, each with its named detail; **`?id=`** returns that one row, and an id that is not a positive whole number is refused with 400.
- **`GET /portal/api/stale`** (`src/portal-stale.ts`) returns `PortalStale`, uncached: up to 200 stale jobs from every namespace with their rule and reason, `truncated`, and a `note` when rows may be missing. It calls `staleJobs` (`src/stale-jobs.ts`), the reader behind `capsid_work_jobs` action `list` with `stale: true`, so it makes one D1 read and one KV read and no GitHub read, and writes nothing.
- **`GET /portal/api/plan`** and **`GET /portal/api/digest?since=`** (`src/portal-overnight.ts`) return `PortalPlan` and `PortalDigest`, uncached, over every namespace: the readers behind `capsid_work_jobs` action `list` view `plan` and view `digest` (`src/overnight-plan.ts`), so the Portal and chat cannot disagree. A `since` that is not an ISO time is a text 400. Both read D1 only and write nothing.
- **`GET /portal/api/convergence`** (`src/portal-convergence.ts`) returns `PortalConvergence`, uncached: each site that opted into its operator API, with its `sync_status` and health checks read live, the repair each check maps to, the watcher's last read of its Worker's secret names, and its last five repairs. It calls only the site's read and its public health route; a repair is the `site_repair` control.
- **`GET /portal/api/maintenance`** (`src/portal-maintenance.ts`) returns `PortalMaintenance`, uncached: the list the daily maintenance pass stored, the one `capsid_run_improve` action `status` serves, with `generated` null before the first pass, and `read`, the open pull requests, branches, repos, compared deploys and driver disk readings the pass read. It reads KV only.
- **`GET /portal/api/claims`** (`src/portal-claims.ts`) returns the per-agent aggregate, filtered by `namespace`, `agent`, `since` and `until` (ISO times; anything else is a text 400), or with `?job=<id>` one job's claims, checks and touches, and a JSON 404 for a job that does not exist. It reads through the `capsid_admin_read_claims` tool's readers and writes nothing.
- **`GET /portal/api/packages/history?name=`** (`src/portal-packages.ts`) returns one configured package's daily downloads, joined to its former name's, and its weekly GitHub rows (`PortalPackageHistory`). A name that is not configured is a 404, so the route cannot fetch an arbitrary package from npm.
- **`GET /portal/api/push`** and **`POST /portal/api/push`** (`src/push.ts`), the phone alerts above. The GET returns `PortalPush`: the VAPID public key (null, with the reason, when the key is not set), the devices with alerts on (never an endpoint), and `digest`, the daily digest's state ("Daily digest", above). The POST takes `{op: "on", subscription, label}`, `{op: "off", id}` or `{op: "test"}` behind the preview's checks (same-origin, the body cap, CSRF), and returns `PortalPush`. Refusals: 400 a bad subscription or op, 409 a device already off or ten already on, 503 no key.
- **`POST /portal/api/sign-out`**, above.
- **Any other `/portal/api/` path** is a JSON 404 behind the gate, never the app's page.
- **`/portal/`** and everything under it serves the built app from the `ASSETS` binding, only after the gate. The page gets `no-store` and its own CSP (scripts, styles and fetches from this origin only); hashed files under `/portal/assets/` are cached privately for a year. A browser loading an unknown path as a page gets the app's page, so the app's own routes load; a missing file stays a 404. With no `ASSETS` binding the admin gets a 503 saying the Portal is not deployed.
- **Deploy config.** The Worker's `assets` block must set `run_worker_first: true` and `not_found_handling: "none"`. Without `run_worker_first`, Cloudflare serves any request that matches a file in the assets directory (its `/index.html` and `/assets/*`, at the root of the origin) without running the Worker, so without the gate. The integration pool calls the Worker directly, not through the assets router, so no request-level test can see the setting; `test/dry-run-config.test.ts` fails instead if `wrangler.jsonc.example` loses either value.
- **The live check.** `scripts/verify-live.mjs` gate 6 asserts `/portal/` answers an anonymous caller with the sign-in (302), and that `/console`, `/console/app/` and `/console/callback` each answer 404. Gate 7 asserts that https://portal.dustinedwards.info/ and its `/mcp` answer an anonymous caller with a 302 to the Access team domain: never the app, and never the MCP endpoint. It reads `PORTAL_ORIGIN` when set.

**What needs Dustin, per app.** GET /ops/inbox (src/inbox.ts) answers, for each configured app (the ops_sites rows, plus any namespace with something waiting), a count, a severity (needs-you, failing or none) and the items behind it: blocked jobs and questions, pull requests auto-merge declined, a failing CI run, a site that is down. It is read by each app's server with a key, not by a browser: a key scoped to one namespace sees that app alone, the admin sees every app, and the response carries no CORS headers and is cached for 30 seconds privately. `capsid_run_improve` action `status` carries the same answer per namespace as needs_dustin. What only the app knows (Carrel's drafts waiting) it reports itself: POST /ops/inbox/report (src/inbox-report.ts; design capsid/research/design-inbox-report.md, Dustin's answers 2026-10-10) with its own key, which must hold the write grant on the namespace the body names. The body is `{ "namespace": "...", "items": [{ "title", "link"?, "since"? }] }`: at most 20 items, titles up to 200 characters, a link only on the app's own ops_sites origin, no severity field (a reported item always counts as needs-you, so an app cannot quiet its own badge). Each report replaces the last whole and expires after 6 hours, so an app that stops reporting stops being counted; send `items: []` to clear. One report per minute per key, refused with 429, and 503 when the counter cannot be read. The inbox lists the items as kind `report`; `capsid_run_improve` action `status` relays each title inside an external fence (src/provenance.ts), since an agent reads it. Tests: test/inbox.test.ts and test-integration/inbox.test.ts.
